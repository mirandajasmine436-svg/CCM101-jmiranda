Reflection

This first mission honestly felt like stepping into the real world of IT. As a Junior Cloud Infrastructure Engineer Trainee at CloudNova Technologies, I got to experience what it's like working inside a Linux environment—something I've only read about before. Using KillerCoda gave me a safe space to practice without worrying about breaking anything, which made the whole learning process less intimidating.

At first, the command line felt a bit overwhelming. I had to constantly check what each command does, but as I kept practicing with `pwd`, `ls`, `mkdir`, and `touch`, things slowly started to click. I remember the small victory I felt when I successfully created my first folder and navigated through directories without messing up. Gathering system information using `lscpu`, `free -h`, and `df -h` also made me realize how much valuable data you can pull from a few simple commands—data that cloud engineers rely on every day to monitor and manage systems.

Setting up my GitHub repository was another highlight. I've always known that version control is important, but actually using `git add`, `git commit`, and `git push` made me appreciate how essential it is for keeping work organized and trackable. Writing documentation in Markdown was surprisingly enjoyable too—it gave me a chance to present my work clearly and professionally.

I won't lie, there were moments of confusion. I occasionally mistyped commands or forgot the right syntax, and I had to double-check my work to avoid moving files to the wrong place. But those small mistakes taught me to slow down, be patient, and pay attention to details—habits that I know will serve me well in the long run.

What I loved most about this mission is how it felt like actual on-the-job training. Every checkpoint—from exploring the environment to building my workspace and documenting everything—prepared me for what real cloud engineers do on a daily basis. It wasn't just about completing tasks; it was about building habits that will stick with me throughout my career.

Looking back, I'm genuinely proud of how much I've learned in such a short time. I'm more confident navigating the Linux terminal, managing files, and using Git. I'm excited to continue this journey and see how much more I can grow as I tackle more complex topics like networking, automation, and cloud platforms.

This portfolio is more than just a requirement—it's a reflection of my progress. I plan to keep updating it after every lab, not just to document what I've done, but to remind myself of how far I've come.
